Vessel Science

The Anatomy of the Brewing Vessel

A tea vessel is not neutral. Its clay porosity, wall thickness, and lid fit dictate thermal decay and taste profile.

01 / PORCELAIN

The Gaiwan (Bowl with Lid)

Vitrified non-porous surface. Absorbs zero essential oils, providing 100% objective clarity. Best suited for high-aroma white teas, green teas, and sensory cupping.

Thermal Retention: Medium • Porosity: 0%
02 / PURPLE STONEWARE

Banko-Ware Kyusu

Fired in reduction kilns with high iron content. The iron interacts with astringent catechins to soften bitter notes and elevate savory l-theanine. Perfect for Sencha and Gyokuro.

Thermal Retention: High • Porosity: 3-5%
03 / ZISHA CLAY

Yixing Purple Clay Pot

Double-pore microstructure breathes while retaining intense heat. Over decades, the pot absorbs tea oils and develops a gleaming luster. Dedicated to a single tea class (Pu-erh or Rock Oolong).

Thermal Retention: Exceptional • Porosity: 8-12%
04 / BOROSILICATE

Glass Cha Hai

Allows visual appreciation of leaf unfurling and exact liquor clarity. Used between teapot and cups to ensure every guest receives identical liquor density.

Thermal Retention: Low • Neutral Visualizer
Hydro-Chemistry

The Hidden Soul of Water

Tea is 99.4% water. Brewing with chlorinated or high-mineral hard tap water will instantly flatten the delicate top notes of first-flush harvests.

TDS Guideline: 30 – 50 ppm Total Dissolved Solids above 100 ppm dulls sweetness; distilled 0 ppm water tastes flat.
pH Balance: 6.8 – 7.2 (Neutral) Alkaline water darkens liquor and precipitates tea polyphenols.

Ancient Three Boil Stages (Lu Yu):

  • • Shrimp Eyes (75°C): Gentle quiet pinhead bubbles on kettle floor. Ideal for Gyokuro & Silver Needle.
  • • Crab Eyes (85°C): Rising bead bubbles clinging to walls. Ideal for Sencha & Darjeeling.
  • • Raging Torrent (98°C+): Billowing surface waves. Ideal for Wuyi Rock Oolongs & Pu-erh.
Sommelier Tip

"Never re-boil stale water. Re-boiling depletes dissolved oxygen, choking the aroma before extraction begins."

Methodology Contrast

Two Philosophies of Extraction

Compare the fast multi-steep Gongfu Cha ceremony with the comforting volume of traditional Western teapot infusion.

Parameter Gongfu Cha Method Western Infusion Method
Leaf to Water Ratio High Leaf Density (5g – 8g per 120ml) Low Leaf Density (2g – 3g per 250ml)
Steeping Duration Flash Steeps (15s to 35s per round) Single Long Steep (3 to 5 minutes)
Infusion Count 6 to 12 consecutive steeps 1 to 2 steeps maximum
Flavor Trajectory Evolving symphony where each cup differs Homogeneous single-layer cup
Vessels Required Gaiwan / Yixing, Fairness Pitcher, Tasting Cups Porcelain Teapot, Mesh Strainer, Mug
Best Suited Teas Wuyi Rock Oolong, Dan Cong, Pu-erh, Gyokuro Darjeeling Black, English Breakfast, Herbal Blends

"Gongfu means 'skill through practice'. It is not about strict dogma, but about training your senses to recognize when the leaf has revealed its soul."

12-HOUR MIZUDASHI EXTRACTION Near-Zero Tannin • Peak L-Theanine
Summer Chilled Extraction

The Art of Mizudashi

When tea leaves are extracted with chilled water over 8 to 12 hours, bitter tannins and astringent catechins do not dissolve. Instead, sweet amino acids and volatile fruity aromatics are gently released.

1. Measure Ratio: 10g whole loose leaf per 1,000ml chilled water
2. Water Quality: Cold spring water or ice cubes placed on dry leaves
3. Chill Duration: Refrigerate sealed bottle at 4°C for 8 to 12 hours
4. Decant & Strain: Strain completely; keeps fresh in glass bottle for 4 days
