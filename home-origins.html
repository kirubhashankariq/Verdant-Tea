Sanctuaries of Camellia Sinensis

Tea is an archive of its environment. Four distinct geological sanctuaries provide the singular conditions necessary for our reserve collections.

Elevation: 380m – 520m

Soil: Humic allophanic loam

Altitude Science

Microclimates of Flavor

As altitude rises, ambient temperatures plummet and oxygen thins. The Camellia sinensis bush grows at half its lowland speed, forcing nutrients into densely packed buds.

400m – 800m Lowland River Mist
  • • Mean Temp: 18°C – 24°C
  • • Growth Rate: Fast / Tender
  • • Compound Profile: High chlorophyll, rich theanine amino acids
  • • Ideal Harvest: Japanese Gyokuro, Tencha, Early Sencha
Sweet Spot 1,000m – 1,500m Cloud Canopy Mid-Ridge
  • • Mean Temp: 12°C – 18°C
  • • Growth Rate: Moderate / Dense
  • • Compound Profile: Balanced polyphenols, complex floral terpene formation
  • • Ideal Harvest: Wuyi Rock Oolongs, Alishan Oolong
1,800m – 2,200m Alpine Summit Ridges
  • • Mean Temp: 5°C – 12°C
  • • Growth Rate: Extreme Slow Pluck
  • • Compound Profile: High antioxidant catechins, muscatel esters
  • • Ideal Harvest: Darjeeling First Flush, High Cloud Dianhong

Living Traditions

Generations of Hand-Picking

Industrial machinery shears stems, twigs, and bruised leaves indiscriminately. Our single-origin partners reject mechanized cutting entirely.

Every leaf in our collection is plucked by master artisans using the thumb-and-forefinger snap technique. This leaves the mother bush completely unharmed and ensures zero oxidation occurs until the withering mats are reached.

Zero Bruising Intact stem seal prevents premature fermentation
Dawn Plucking Plucked between 5:30 AM and 8:00 AM before noon heat

Ecosystem Integrity

Living Soils & Agro-Forestry

Commercial monoculture strips mountain topsoils of their ancestral microbial communities. Our garden stewards practice complete permaculture, interplanting tea bushes with wild camphor, nitrogen-fixing alder trees, and indigenous flowers.

The Solar Calendar

The Solar Terms of Tea

Following the 24 ancient agricultural solar terms. Each window represents a brief 10 to 14 day atmospheric inflection when leaves capture peak character.

TERM: CHUNFEN (MAR 20 - APR 5)

Pre-Qingming Spring Buds

Plucked prior to the spring rains when weather is crisp and cold. Yields the highest market price and unmatched clarity.

• Silver Needle & Longjing
TERM: GUYU (APR 20 - MAY 5)

First Flush (Shincha)

The foundational spring flush of Japan. Full leaf emergence rich with volatile grassy aromatics and deep chlorophyll sweetness.

• Uji Gyokuro & Sencha
TERM: MANGZHONG (JUN 5 - JUN 20)

Summer Muscatel Flush

Intense Himalayan sunshine warms the terraced hills. Leafhoppers gently nibble leaves, stimulating natural muscatel perfume.

• Darjeeling Second Flush
TERM: BAI LU (SEP 8 - OCT 5)

Autumn Dew Roasting

Leaves mature through late summer warmth. Ideal for deep charcoal roasting and long resting periods inside clay jars.

• Wuyi Yan Cha & Hojicha
